Why Queuecraft was built first, out of the 26 ideas in the brief.

## Workspace context

`Projects-Experiments` already holds several cinematic, narrative websites (DarkRoom, Atlas of Unfinished Places,
Registry of Cast Shade, Bureau of Impossible Phenomena), three portfolio sites, PRIMORDIA (evolved creatures in 3D,
with an ecosystem planned), SERI (a card game), a CHIP-8 emulator, Pong and Snake, and BAPE-LAB (notes on how
processes, memory and networking work on Windows). There was no interactive systems simulation and no developer tool.

## Five different questions, five different answers

- **Can build:** nearly all 26.
- **Easiest to finish at high quality:** JSON Lens, Token Garden, Pixel Forensics.
- **Most striking visually:** Voidline, Generative Art Archive, Echoform.
- **Most portfolio value:** Queuecraft, Packet Odyssey, Repo Atlas.
- **Best to build first:** Queuecraft.

## Decision

Queuecraft is the only candidate near the top of every column except raw visual impact, and its visuals come from
the system itself rather than decoration. It fits the owner's stated interest ("knowing what the machine is doing
underneath"), fills the empty spot in the workspace, and its core claim (this policy behaves like this under this
failure) can be checked by tests rather than by eye.

**Kept out of the MVP on purpose:** multiple queues or priorities, autoscaling, dependency overload feedback
(metastable failure), visibility-timeout duplicates, saving runs, a scenario editor, sound.

**Done meant:** six scenarios whose card text is verified by tests; every control applies live; faults, compare with
the fix, job trace, DLQ redrive and share links work end to end in a real browser on desktop and phone widths;
`npm run verify` and the e2e suite pass.
